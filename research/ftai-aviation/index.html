The short in brief

FTAI Aviation buys, leases and rebuilds CFM56 jet engines. Its Aerospace Products business swaps in rebuilt engine modules in 5 to 25 days at a fixed price, against 90 to 150 days for a full shop visit. A new Power business turns retired CFM56 cores into 25 MW turbines for data centers.

The Street values FTAI as a fast-growing aftermarket disruptor: 1,700 modules at a 32% margin in 2027, plus about 60 power units earned that year. We think the edges behind its old ~35% margin are wearing down. The cheap engines it once owned are used up, so it now buys cores at market. The work is getting heavier as engines arrive for their second shop visit. And technicians, not demand, cap how many modules it can build.

Power is real but books late. About a third of each unit’s profit waits for customer acceptance, so we expect 31 of 60 units to count in 2027. The trade was not crowded: short interest was 6.1% of the float with 4.0 days to cover, and no analyst rated the stock a sell.

Where we differ from the Street

ThesisStreet viewOur view

Thesis 1The work is getting heavier

Street view

Heavy repair work is a temporary share grab, and the Aerospace margin rebuilds to 32% as volume scales.

Our view

Engines are arriving for their second shop visit short of life-limited parts, and FTAI prices under CFM’s list at a fixed price, so it absorbs the overruns. Each new Aerospace dollar earned 22 to 26% in 2026. We model a 28.0% margin for 2027.

Thesis 2Growth relies on bought cores and partner labor

Street view

Aerospace Products is a compounder: 1,700 modules in 2027 and about $1.5 billion of EBITDA, worth 17 times.

Our view

Owned lease engines fell from 312 to 145, and 87% of cores are now bought at market. Technicians cap 2027 at about 1,408 modules, and 76% of new capacity is run by partners or a joint venture at someone else’s margin.

Thesis 3Power is priced as a franchise but books late

Street view

Power is a second compounder: 100+ units under contract and about 60 units at $7.5 million each in 2027, worth 18 to 22 times EBITDA.

Our view

A first-year ramp that books late: a third of each unit’s profit waits for customer acceptance, about 4.5 months after handover. That leaves about $280 million of 2027 Power EBITDA, not $450 million, worth 8 to 12 times.

How we built the view

The work behind the thesis, beyond consensus models and company guidance.

  • Airline bond filings

    We read appraisers’ condition reports for 100 engines in US airline bond (EETC) filings to measure how much life-limited part life engines have left at their second visit.

  • Lease and contract exhibits

    We pulled 16 engine lease exhibits from SEC filings: all restrict cheaper PMA parts, which limits FTAI’s cost levers.

  • FAA registry scrape

    FTAI-linked tail numbers appear in the FAA registry 68 to 170 days before teardown, a lead indicator for engine supply.

  • Chinese-language filings

    Jereh’s filings show J&F is a controlling subsidiary of Jereh and that FTAI is one of four turbine suppliers, which reframes the $1.465bn Power order.

  • Re-derived disclosures

    From 10-Q notes we rebuilt the share of engine feedstock bought at market (17% to 81% in a year) and cost per module (+29% in two quarters).

  • Labor capacity model

    Technician headcount, modules per head and an hours factor for heavier builds cap 2027 output at about 1,408 modules.

  • Expert and counterparty checks

    Expert calls, a former GE Vernova executive on turbine ramps, and GE’s CFO on the shortage of used engines.

  • AI-assisted research, red-teamed

    A five-step agentic workflow using Claude, with every fact tagged as verified, derived, secondary or unverified, unverified claims kept off slides, and five bull rebuttals written against our own case.

Valuation

Our one-year target of $131.42 is the base-case DCF, using a 10.84% WACC and a 10.8x exit multiple on 2027 EBITDA, in line with cost-plus engine repair peers. Weighting the three scenarios 25/50/25 gives $142.40, still 21% below the price. Even a bull case that matches Street EPS is worth only 21% more, so the base-case downside is about 1.3 times the bull-case upside.

Catalysts

  • 10 Dec 2026

    Investor Day

    The Street needs about 425 modules a quarter against a third-quarter record of 311. We win if there are no price escalators, the Aerospace margin is guided near 30% and there is no direct Power order.

  • About 24 Dec 2026

    Department of Energy rules

    New rules may restrict turbines tied to foreign-controlled firms, and J&F, the Power sales channel, is controlled by Jereh. We win if J&F units need a review or licence.

  • Late Feb 2027

    Fourth-quarter results

    The 2026 guide needs fourth-quarter Aerospace EBITDA of $295M at a 31% margin. We model about $281M as fund work reloads and overruns build.

  • Late Feb 2027

    2027 guidance and 10-K

    The Street’s $1,505M of Aerospace EBITDA meets management’s ~$1.4bn and our $1,260M, and the first Power segment line shows handovers against acceptances.

  • Apr to May 2027

    First Power quarter

    We expect 8 handovers and about $31M of Power EBITDA against a ~$112M run-rate. We win if first-quarter modules come in at 330 or fewer.
